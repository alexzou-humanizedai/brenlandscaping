How much does a paver patio cost in Fairfield County, CT?

By Dennis Leahy, Owner Updated July 2026 6 min read
Quick Answer

In 2026, most paver patios in Fairfield County run $25–$45 per square foot installed. Natural bluestone runs $35–$60 per square foot. A typical 300–400 sq ft patio lands between $8,000 and $18,000, with site access, excavation, and drainage driving where you fall in that range.

"What's this going to cost?" is the first question I get on almost every walk-through, so let's answer it honestly. Every yard is different — that's not a dodge, it's why the ranges are wide — but after years of building patios around Fairfield, Westport, and Trumbull, the numbers below are what real projects actually cost here.

What actually drives the price?

  • The base, not the stone. Most of a patio's cost — and all of its longevity — is underground: excavation, 6–10 inches of compacted gravel, and correct pitch for drainage. A cheap quote usually means a thin base, and a thin base means a wavy patio in three Connecticut winters.
  • Material choice. Concrete pavers are economical and consistent. Natural bluestone costs more but is the classic New England look and ages beautifully.
  • Site access. If we can get a machine to the site, excavation is fast. Tight side yards or backyards with no equipment access mean hand work, which adds labor days.
  • Drainage and grading. Fairfield County lots often slope or hold water. Fixing that as part of the project costs money — ignoring it costs the patio.
  • Extras that make the space. Firepits, sitting walls, steps, and lighting each add to the budget, but they're also what turn a slab of stone into a place you actually spend evenings.

Pavers or bluestone — which should you pick?

Pick concrete pavers if budget predictability matters most, you like a uniform look, and you may expand later — matching pavers is easy. Pick bluestone if you want the timeless Connecticut look, plan to stay in the house, or the patio adjoins older stonework. Resale-wise, both are strong; done well, bluestone tends to photograph — and appraise — a touch better in our market.

What a good quote should include

Whoever you hire — us or anyone else — a written patio quote should spell out: excavation depth and base material, drainage plan, the exact stone by name, edge restraint, joint treatment (polymeric sand vs. stone dust), cleanup and haul-away, and a start window. If a quote is just a number on a page, ask questions before you sign it.

Common questions

Do I need a permit for a patio in Fairfield County?

A ground-level patio usually doesn't require a building permit in most local towns, but retaining walls over a certain height, roofed structures, and work near wetlands can. We confirm requirements with your town before any project starts.

How long does installation take?

Most 300–400 sq ft patios take one to two weeks on site, depending on access and weather. Bluestone and specialty pavers can add a few weeks of material lead time, so plan a season ahead for summer deadlines.

Is it cheaper to build in the fall?

Sometimes. Late fall is a slower season for masonry crews in Connecticut, so scheduling is easier and pricing can be sharper. The ground just needs to stay workable before hard freeze.
