When is the best time to plant trees and shrubs in Connecticut?

By Dennis Leahy, Owner Updated July 2026 5 min read
Quick Answer

Early fall — mid-September through late October — is the best time to plant trees and shrubs in Connecticut. The soil is still warm, the air is cooling, and roots get a full head start before summer stress. Early spring (April to mid-May) is a close second.

Every spring my phone fills up with planting requests, and every fall I plant the projects that will actually have the easiest first year. It feels backwards — garden centers are busiest in May — but fall planting works with our climate instead of against it. Here's the reasoning, season by season.

Why fall is the sweet spot

In September and October, Connecticut soil is still holding summer warmth while the air cools off. That combination is exactly what a transplant wants: roots grow eagerly in warm soil, and the plant isn't fighting heat or drought up top. By the time the ground freezes, a fall-planted tree has months of root growth banked. When summer arrives the following year, it's established — not struggling through its first heat wave three weeks after leaving the nursery pot.

Spring planting still works — with a catch

April through mid-May is a perfectly good planting window, and for a few plants it's the better one (see below). The catch is what follows: a spring-planted shrub hits July with a root system that's only a few months old. That means diligent watering all summer — miss two hot weeks in August and you can lose the plant. If you plant in spring, budget for the watering, not just the plants.

What about summer and winter?

Summer planting is possible with container-grown stock, heavy mulch, and near-daily water, but I only recommend it when a construction schedule demands it. Winter is for planning: it's when we design, so plants are ordered and ready the moment the ground wakes up.

A quick season-by-season guide

  • Mid-September – late October: Best window for most deciduous trees, shrubs, and evergreens. Aim to get evergreens in by mid-October so they root before winter wind.
  • April – mid-May: Best for borderline-hardy plants (crape myrtle, some hydrangeas), bare-root stock, and anything marginal in a cold winter.
  • May – June: Perennials and annuals; fine for shrubs if you'll water faithfully.
  • November: Spring bulbs, and last call for hardy deciduous trees while the ground is still workable.

The first year decides everything

More new plantings die from watering problems than from wrong timing. The rule we give every client: a slow, deep soak at the root ball two to three times a week for the first growing season, two to three inches of mulch kept off the trunk, and water for evergreens right up until the ground freezes. Do that, and even a spring planting sails through.

Common questions

Can you plant trees in summer in Connecticut?

Yes, but July and August heat is hard on new roots and demands near-daily watering. If the calendar forces it, choose container-grown stock and commit to the hose — or wait for September.

What hardiness zone is Fairfield County?

Mostly USDA zone 7a, with colder inland pockets closer to 6b. Coastal towns like Fairfield and Westport get a moderating effect from Long Island Sound.

How often should I water new plantings?

Deeply, two to three times a week for the first season — a slow soak beats a daily sprinkle. Taper in fall; keep watering evergreens until freeze-up.
